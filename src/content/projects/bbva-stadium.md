## Project Overview

This report analyzes the **BBVA Stadium**, popularly known as the "Gigante de Acero" (Steel Giant), located in Guadalupe, Monterrey. The study evaluates its architectural integration with nature and its status as a sustainable landmark in Latin America.

## Biophilic Design Analysis

The stadium incorporates several patterns of biophilic design to enhance the connection between users and the natural environment:

* **Visual Connection with Nature:** Integration of grass and trees in the exterior and specialized green parking areas.


* **Thermal & Airflow Variations:** An asymmetric design that allows for natural air currents and sunlight entry.


* **Biomorphic Forms:** The complex, organic shape of the structure evokes mystery and a natural aesthetic.


* **Water Presence:** Exterior water jets provide non-rhythmic sensory stimuli to visitors.



## LEED Certification

The BBVA Stadium was the first in Latin America to receive **LEED Silver certification** from the U.S. Green Building Council. The certification process involves six key stages:

1. Classification system selection.


2. Meeting location and size prerequisites.


3. Establishing registration and certification deadlines.


4. Fee payment based on project scale.


5. Team assembly (including owners and administrators).


6. Final registration and inspection for certification.



## Sustainability Proposals

To further enhance the stadium's ecological footprint and visitor experience, several improvements were proposed:

* **Interior Vegetation:** Implementing shade plants within the stadium to improve oxygen circulation and sensory experience.


* **Natural Textures:** Covering grey concrete support columns with wood or materials resembling trees.


* **Sensory Integration:** Utilizing natural scents and ambient environmental sounds to reduce stress and improve mood.


* **Biodiversity:** Creating exterior ponds with marine plants and fish to increase local biodiversity.
